# How many applied for PKSF Software Engineer (Full-stack)?

Estimate made 2026-11-16. **PKSF has not published an applicant count, and no public social-media engagement figures were available**: the news articles show share buttons without counts, and Facebook posts cannot be read by automated tools. This is a reasoned estimate, not a measurement.

## Hard facts found

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| **16 posts** of Software Engineer (Full-stack), plus 4 Senior Software Engineer (Full-stack) | Prothom Alo and The Daily Campus, both 8 Sep 2026 |
| Salary BDT 1,20,000/month (senior: 1,60,000) | same |
| 27 posts in total across 7 IT roles; online application; deadline 23 Sep 2026 | same |
| Widely circulated: national daily, job portals, aggregator sites, bdjobs, LinkedIn reposts | search results |
| Filters (from the ToR): age ≤ 32, ≥ 2 years full-stack experience, three first divisions/classes, no third division, Spring Boot + React/Angular | ToR.pdf |

## What pushes the number up

- **The pay is very high** for a 2+ years-experience role in Bangladesh, and it comes with public-sector-style stability and allowances.
- **16 vacancies** is unusually many for one specialised IT post, which encourages people to try.
- Wide media coverage, an easy online application, and no hard technical screen at application time.
- Many people apply even when they don't fully meet the experience or academic filters.

## What pulls it down

- Three filters cut the eligible pool sharply: **≥ 2 years of full-stack experience, age ≤ 32, and three first divisions/classes**.
- The specific stack: Java/Spring Boot + React/Angular, not just "any developer".
- Experienced developers in good private jobs, especially those earning in foreign currency remotely, may not apply.

## Estimate

| Stage | Range (best guess) |
|---|---|
| Applications for Software Engineer (Full-stack) | **≈ 1,500 – 4,000** (central guess ~2,500) |
| Called to the written exam after screening | **≈ 600 – 2,000** |
| Written candidates per post (16 posts) | **≈ 40 – 120 per post** |

How the estimate works: attractive, widely advertised Bangladeshi IT posts with experience filters typically draw low thousands of applications. Unfiltered entry-level and civil-service posts draw tens of thousands or more; national BCS exams draw hundreds of thousands. The experience and academic filters put this post well below those, while the salary and 16 vacancies put it above a typical single specialised vacancy.

**What it means for you:** you probably need to be in roughly the **top 5–10%** of written-exam candidates to reach the viva for one of the 16 posts. Some posts may also be filled by people who apply to both the senior and regular roles. This supports the strategy already in the app: aim for consistently high written scores, not a pass.

## How to get a much better number yourself

1. **Your admit card roll number.** If roll numbers are sequential (common in Bangladeshi recruitment), the range across the seat plan shows the number of candidates. Compare your number with others you know who applied.
2. **The seat plan** (often sent by SMS or posted on recruitment.pksf.org.bd): number of rooms × seats per room ≈ written-exam candidates.
3. **On exam day:** count the rooms and seats at the venue.
4. **The written result:** PKSF usually publishes passing roll numbers. Comparing passers with the roll range gives the pass rate for the viva stage.
5. Ask in Bangladeshi developer or job-preparation groups where others share their roll numbers.

## Did PKSF shortlist before the written exam? (added 2026-11-16)

**Can't be confirmed.** The circular page on recruitment.pksf.org.bd now shows "Access Restricted / Page Not Found", and the portal lists no circulars, so its exact wording about shortlisting couldn't be read.

**Most likely: yes, at least an eligibility screening.**
- The email says PKSF is "glad to invite you to appear at the Written Examination", sent individually through the applicant dashboard. That reads as a selective invitation, not an automatic one.
- The ToR's mandatory criteria are hard and machine-checkable from the online form: age ≤ 32 on the last date, at least 2 years of full-stack experience, three first divisions/classes, no third division, and the degree subject. Filtering on these is routine in online recruitment systems.
- **A competitive CV shortlist (only the top N) is less likely.** With 16 vacancies and a written exam built to rank candidates, organisations usually invite everyone eligible and let the exam do the ranking. A competitive shortlist is more typical when there is no written test.

**What it means:**
- Everyone in the exam hall has probably passed the same filters: 2+ years of full-stack work, strong academics, age ≤ 32. The competition is qualified, not a crowd of random applicants.
- Screening removes the many ineligible applications, so written candidates are likely at the **lower end** of the earlier range: roughly **500–1,500**, or about **30–90 per post**.

**How to check:**
1. Ask others who applied. If someone with under 2 years of experience, or a third division, was not invited, there was screening.
2. Check your dashboard for a status such as "Shortlisted" or "Eligible", and re-read the circular/ToR saved there for a line like "only short-listed candidates will be called".
3. The roll-number range on admit cards and the seat plan still give the real count.
