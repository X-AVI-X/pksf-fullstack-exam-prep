Q: The CIA triad stands for:
* Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability
- Control, Identity, Access
- Cryptography, Integrity, Authentication
- Confidentiality, Identity, Authorization
E: Encryption protects confidentiality, hashes and signatures protect integrity, and redundancy plus DDoS protection protect availability.
Q: Symmetric vs asymmetric encryption?
* Symmetric uses one shared key (AES) and is fast; asymmetric uses a public/private key pair (RSA, ECC) for key exchange and signatures
- Asymmetric is faster for bulk data
- Symmetric uses two keys
- They are the same
E: TLS uses asymmetric crypto to agree a session key, then symmetric AES-GCM for the data.
Q: Authentication vs authorization?
* Authentication verifies who you are; authorization decides what you may do
- They are the same
- Authorization comes first
- Authentication assigns roles
E: Keycloak login is authentication. RBAC checks such as @PreAuthorize("hasRole('ADMIN')") are authorization. HTTP 401 means not authenticated; 403 means authenticated but forbidden.
Q: A digital signature provides:
* Integrity, authenticity and non-repudiation: signed with the private key, verified with the public key
- Confidentiality only
- Compression
- Availability
E: A JWT signed with RS256 works this way: Keycloak signs with its private key and services verify with the public JWKS.
Q: Which attack tricks a logged-in user's browser into sending an unwanted state-changing request?
- XSS
* CSRF
- SQL injection
- Brute force
E: Defend against CSRF with SameSite cookies, CSRF tokens, or bearer tokens in headers instead of cookies. XSS injects script into pages; defend with output encoding and CSP.
Q: Why add a salt before hashing a password?
* So identical passwords produce different hashes, defeating precomputed rainbow tables
- To make hashing reversible
- To encrypt the password
- To shorten the hash
E: BCrypt and Argon2 generate and store a salt automatically and are deliberately slow.
Q: The principle of least privilege means:
* Each user, service and DB account gets only the permissions it needs
- Admins get all rights by default
- Everyone shares one account
- Permissions are granted for life
E: Example: the reporting service connects with a read-only DB role, and Kubernetes service accounts have minimal RBAC.
Q: MFA combines factors from different categories, such as:
* Something you know (password) + something you have (OTP device) + something you are (biometric)
- Two passwords
- Password and username
- Two security questions
E: Two items from the same category is not true MFA. Keycloak supports OTP and WebAuthn.
